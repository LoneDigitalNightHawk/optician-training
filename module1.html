Module 1 of the VHDO Training Curriculum — Welcome & Corporate Anatomy

Welcome to Visual Health Doctors of Optometry. Before you learn a single lens term, you should know what this company is, how it's organised, who does what, and where you fit. That's this module: the anatomy of VHDO. It's short on purpose — read it on your first day, take the quizzes, and you'll walk into your office knowing whose name to call for which problem. Chapter 1

Welcome to VHDO

Visual Health Doctors of Optometry is a doctor-led optometry group with eleven offices across Northern Virginia. Patients come to us first for eye care — comprehensive exams, medical visits, contact lens fittings — and then for the eyewear that comes out of that care.

That order matters, and it shapes everything about how we work. In a recent two-week period the company wrote 1,215 orders for $590,197 net, and the single largest share of that revenue was professional services — the doctors' exams, not merchandise. We are a healthcare practice with a dispensary attached, not a glasses store with a doctor in the back. Patients feel that difference, and it's why they trust the recommendations we make.

Three things we expect from everyone, in every role, from day one:

  • Never guess in front of a patient. "Let me find out" is a professional answer. A confident wrong answer is the only real mistake in this company.
  • Treat patient information as medical information — because it is. What you see on a ticket, a chart, or a screen stays inside these walls.
  • Finish the visit. Every patient should leave with their care complete — exam done, questions answered, eyewear handled, next step booked. Half-finished visits are where patients, and revenue, quietly leak away.

The habit to build: route the question, don't absorb it. When a patient asks something outside your lane — "is this cataract serious?", "why did my insurance deny this?" — your job is a warm handoff to the right person, not an improvised answer. Patients respect "that's a question for the doctor, let me get them" far more than a guess.

"That's really a question for Dr. — let me grab them before you leave so you get the right answer, not my best guess."

Chapter 4

The Systems You'll Touch

Four systems run this company. You'll be trained on each in depth later in the curriculum — for now, know what each one is and what lives inside it.

  • Eyefinity Encompass — our EPM (electronic practice management). Patient records, appointments, exams, orders, tickets, payments. Everything you sell and everything the doctor documents flows through it. Module 5 is devoted entirely to mastering it.
  • The lab ecosystem. Every spectacle order you write becomes a physical job — surfaced, coated, edged, inspected. Where jobs go, how long they take, and why remakes hurt is Module 3.
  • The insurance matrix. Carriers, plans, allowances, authorizations, and the ledger where it all has to reconcile. Module 4 builds this from the ground up with practical ledger work.
  • Trainual. The platform where this curriculum will ultimately live, assign, and track completion for every team member. During the build-out you're reading the web version; the destination is Trainual.

One rule covers all of them: these systems hold live patient and financial data — when you're unsure, stop and ask before you click. An improvised click in Eyefinity can mis-bill a claim or overwrite an order. There is no prize for figuring it out alone; there is a cost for guessing wrong.
